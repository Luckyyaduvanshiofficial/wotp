# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to each other. Critique code, not people. Assume good faith until it is clearly not there.

## The standard

This project is maintained by one person and contributed to by strangers across time zones. Most conversations happen in writing, in a second language for many participants, with no tone of voice to soften anything. That makes clarity and patience load-bearing.

So:

- **Critique the code, not the coder.** "This will double-send on retry" is helpful. "Did you even think about retries?" is not.
- **Assume good faith.** A confusing PR is far more likely to be inexperience or a language gap than malice. Ask before you conclude.
- **Be patient with English.** This project's community skews Indian and international, and many contributors write English as a second, third, or fourth language. Perfect grammar is not a contribution requirement, and nobody should feel they need it to participate. If a report is unclear, ask a clarifying question.
- **Disagree openly, decide gracefully.** Technical disagreement is welcome and useful. Once a decision is made, don't relitigate it in every thread.
- **Say thank you.** Someone spent their evening on this.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussions
- Personal attacks, insults, or dismissiveness aimed at a person rather than their work
- Discriminatory comments or jokes based on race, ethnicity, caste, religion, nationality, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, or level of experience
- Sexualized language, imagery, or attention of any kind
- Publishing someone's private information — real name, address, phone number, employer — without their explicit permission
- Deliberately derailing issues or pull requests, or reopening settled decisions to wear people down
- Using this project's spaces to promote a competing service in bad faith

## Scope

This applies to all project spaces — issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review, and discussions — and to any space where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through GitHub. If the report concerns a specific thread, use the **Report content** option on the relevant comment or issue; for anything else, open a private [security advisory](https://github.com/Luckyyaduvanshiofficial/wotp/security/advisories/new) and mark it as a conduct report — it is the only private channel this repository has, and it works fine for this.

Reports are read by the maintainer alone. The person reported will not be told who reported them.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may take any action proportionate to the behaviour:

1. **A private word** — for a first, minor, or clearly unintentional lapse.
2. **A public correction** — a note in the thread about what was inappropriate.
3. **Removal** — comments, commits, issues, or pull requests may be edited or deleted.
4. **A ban** — temporary or permanent, for repeated or serious violations, without further warning.

Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject any contribution that violates this document. This is not a judgement about anyone's worth as a person, and it is not open to negotiation in the thread it happens in. If you believe a decision was wrong, raise it privately and it will be reconsidered honestly.
